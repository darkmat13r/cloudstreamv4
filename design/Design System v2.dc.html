CLOUDSTREAM 4 · DESIGN SYSTEM v0.4 · GEOMETRY CONTRACT

What the specs found, and then what measuring found.

v0.2 closed the gaps ten adversarially-critiqued specs exposed: a focus ring published without its footprint, a TV ramp with no mobile or desktop sibling, a state policy with no state component. Then the screens were actually drawn and measured — and a second, harder class of defect appeared, invisible to every review that read the source instead of the rendered box. A scanner that checked every px value declared in CSS passed three files that between them held 50 off-grid boxes. §06 is that class. It is the section to read if you read only one. v0.4 adds the one defect that no amount of geometry measurement could ever have found, because it moves nothing: §08.

01 · CORRECTION

v0.1 required every artboard dimension to be a multiple of 4px. But the artboard is 2× dp, and the repo rule (dimension-scale.md) requires every dp to be a multiple of 4 — so every artboard px must be a multiple of 8, not 4. Checking the published ladder against that stricter test, one rung fails.

02 · FOCUS FOOTPRINT

A focus ring occupies 8px outside the thing it rings

The single most expensive omission in v0.1. The ring is a 4px stroke at a 4px offset — 2dp and 2dp — so it bleeds 8px on every side of its target. Nothing in v0.1 said so, and every cluster laid focusables flush to the safe area and 16px apart, which means rings crossing into overscan and adjacent rings touching.

The usable focus band on a 1920×1080 TV artboard is x 56–1864, y 56–1024. That is the 48px safe area plus the 8px ring bleed, and it is measured on the element’s own box — the bleed lives in the 48–56 reserve, which is exactly why the margin is 56 and not 48. One exception, and only one: a single row of cards may cross the bottom as a below-the-fold peek, because reaching it scrolls the region and puts it back inside the band.
03 · THREE RAMPS

One scale per form factor, with its line box

v0.1 defined only the TV ramp, so the specs invented 13sp, 11sp, 15sp, 16sp, 20sp and 24sp across six clusters. v0.2 published three ramps but no line boxes, and that omission cost real clipping: a 64px headline was drawn in a 72px box and lost its descenders, then in an 80px box and still lost them. That fix was itself an estimate — “about 1.3×” — so v0.3 measured the fonts. There are two different heights here and only one of them clips. The ink is the glyph bounding box, measureText().actualBoundingBox: Manrope 1.30× the size at its heaviest weight, JetBrains Mono 1.182×. That is the number that must fit the line box. The content box is the browser’s own reserve and runs larger — 1.36–1.375× for Manrope — but a line box smaller than it only means negative half-leading, which does not clip. Measure ink; ignore the content box. One ratio for both families was the bug: at 20px Manrope inks 26.0 and cannot live in 24, while mono inks 23.64 and can, so the caption rung below is two rows. Take the box from the table, never from a ratio — ink also varies with weight, and the table is worst-case. Never line-height: 1.

Mobile is not TV ÷ 2. A 10-foot ramp halved gives a 10dp caption, which is below the touch floor. Mobile has its own ramp with a 12dp floor, and desktop shares it with one extra display tier. If a size is not in this table it does not exist.

The open question on this page. TV caption is 20px, which is 10sp — below Android TV’s own 12sp legibility minimum. It is recorded here rather than quietly rounded up, because raising it reflows every artboard that uses it. Someone has to decide.

Two rungs look wrong and are correct. Manrope 24/32 and mono 20/24 both have a content box larger than their line box — 33 in 32, and 26 in 24. Neither clips, because the ink is 31.2 and 23.64. Do not “fix” them: raising Manrope 24 to a 40 box would add 8px to every card title and list row in the app to solve a problem that does not exist. This is exactly why the test is ink and not the content box.

What the split caption costs. A sans caption is now 32 tall, not 24, so any stack that pairs it with a 32px title is 8px taller than it was. That is the correct number and the previous one was clipping, but it does reflow: TV Search drew a result title at Manrope 20/24 and measured one clipped run because of it, and :core:theme gave caption and captionMono a single shared CaptionLineHeight, which cannot express the split at all.

05 · STATEVIEW

One component, five variants, two placements

v0.1 mandated five states but gave no component, so the specs produced eleven names for it: StateBlock, StatePanel, InlineStateBlock, ListStateBlock, EmptyState, ErrorState, InlineRetry, InlineErrorStrip, PartialBanner, StatusStrip, NoticeRow. They are one thing.

Two placements, and the difference matters. Replaces-region is for when there is nothing to show. Banner-above-content is for when there is — offline and partial both keep real rows underneath, so a block that replaces the region cannot serve them.

A StateView’s action must be in the screen’s focus order, not merely present. v0.2 said it “always carries exactly one focusable” and stopped there, and eighteen of twenty audited screens then mounted a Retry that no D-pad move could reach. Hang it off a row that already exists — Search puts “Retry failed” at the end of the chip row — rather than floating it in a band of its own, which costs 120px of vertical budget and is what pushes a rail past the band. This is now enforced in code: StatePrompt takes its action as a required parameter, so a StateView without one cannot be constructed.

And the action has to be reachable by geometry, not just present in a list. The suggestions cluster put a state action at x824–1240 with 488px of void above it and nothing else focusable in the region — three of six chips had no DOWN target that reached it at all. “In the focus order” means a real D-pad move lands on it from where focus actually is.

06 · WHAT MEASURING FOUND

Seven rules you cannot see by reading the source

Everything above was found by reviewing. This section was found by rendering the drawn screens and measuring every box with getBoundingClientRect. The declared values were right in all seven cases; where the elements landed was not. A scanner reading declared CSS passed three files holding 50 off-grid boxes; extending the same measurement to text boxes found 15 more — and then found the headings on this page. Rule 7 was found last, by a scanner strict enough to catch what the first six left, and it is the one that says the method itself has to be checked.

The method, and the checks on the method. Verify a design by publishing it, rendering it and measuring computed boxes — off-grid, focus band, clipped text, pairwise collision. Not screenshots, and not by eye. But a scanner is a piece of software with its own bugs, and three of its rules have to be right or it lies in both directions: clipping is measured ink (measureText().actualBoundingBox) against the line box, not scrollHeight and not the content box, which over-reports; the focus band is tested on the element box, never on the ring, or every correctly placed edge element is flagged; and an intrinsic width must be excluded by asking whether a width was declared, because min-width is not a width and a flex item's display is blockified, so both heuristics for it are wrong. A scanner that reports 99 defects of which 90 are its own is worse than none — people stop reading it. The same discipline applies to the Compose implementation, where “pixel-perfect” means asserted bounds rather than a preview that looked right.

08 · CONTRAST

The defect that moves nothing, and so survived every measurement

§06 is a whole section about defects that are invisible in the source and obvious in the rendered box. This one is invisible in both. muted was #686868, and it fails the WCAG AA 4.5:1 floor on all three grounds it is ever drawn on. Seven files measured 0 off-grid, 0 clipped and 0 band violations while every one of them set its secondary text below the legibility minimum, because a colour does not move a box and a geometry scanner has no opinion about it. It is now #858585.

Why 4.5:1 and not 3:1. The 3:1 allowance is for large text — 24px, or 18.66px bold. muted is used at 20 and 24 artboard px, which is 10 and 12 sp. It is small text by the standard’s own definition and it is read at three metres, so if anything the threshold should be stricter, not looser.

Why #858585 and not something rounder. #808080 — the obvious choice — measures 4.47:1 on raised and misses by 0.03. #858585 is the smallest value that clears the floor on all three grounds, and at 5.69:1 it still sits far below ink2’s 13.08 and ink’s 21, so the three-step muted / ink2 / ink hierarchy still reads as three steps rather than collapsing into two.

What is still below the line and is staying there. live is 4.36:1 on black — fine for the 4px progress rule and the playing glyph, which are graphics and owe 3:1, and it is never used for body text by §04’s rules. line is 1.45:1 and is a hairline, not text; where a boundary is the only thing distinguishing a control, that control also carries a raised fill. Both are deliberate; both would be defects the moment someone set a sentence in them.
